# The 2026 Levy (Issue 4) and the Five-Year Forecast

[← Back to the CH-UH schools guide](./)

*Cleveland Heights-University Heights City School District (CH-UH), Ohio. State IRN 043794. Researched October 12, 2026.*

This page sets out what is on the ballot, what the district's own forecasts show, the history of past levies, and the main arguments on each side. OpenHeights does not take a position. Every figure links to its source and gives the date or year it applies to. Our own arithmetic is labeled "OpenHeights calculation."

> **Verification status: checked against primary sources on Oct. 12, 2026**, except items listed under [Gaps / unverified](#gaps--unverified). We checked the certified ballot language (Board of Elections issues list), the district's levy-facts page, the treasurer's forecast assumption documents for October 2025, February 2026 and August 2026 (state forecast portal), the state's forecast data files, the state's FY2025 District Profile Report, the district's FY2025 annual financial report, and county election reports. **One important limit:** the state's data file for the August 2026 forecast repeats the February 2026 numbers for CH-UH (see [below](#a-data-problem-in-the-states-august-file)), so for August 2026 we have only the figures the district itself published, not every line.

## Key facts

- **Ballot:** **Issue 4** on the November 3, 2026 ballot in the CH-UH school district. Confirmed on the [Cuyahoga County Board of Elections issues list][7] (Issue 4, ballot code BCLH10, "Cleve Hts/Univ Hts CSD"). A simple majority is needed to pass.
- **Type:** an **additional** (new) levy for current operating expenses, for a **continuing period** (no end date). It is not a renewal or replacement ([BOE issues list][7]).
- **Rate:** not more than **6.5 mills** ([BOE issues list][7]).
- **Annual revenue:** **$11,100,193** a year, the County Fiscal Officer's estimate in the ballot language ([BOE issues list][7]).
- **Cost to a homeowner:** **$228 a year for each $100,000** of County Fiscal Officer market value ([BOE issues list][7]), about $19 a month ([district levy-facts page][1]).
- **Timing:** It would start in tax year 2026, with the first payments due in calendar year 2027 ([BOE issues list][7]).
- **Board votes:** The board passed the resolution of necessity at a July 21, 2026 special meeting. On August 3, 2026 it voted to put the levy on the ballot under Ohio Revised Code 5705.21 ([district, July 27, 2026][39]; [district, Aug. 24, 2026][4]; [district meeting summary, Aug. 3, 2026][40]). Hoodline reported the August 3 vote as unanimous ([Hoodline][3]); the district posts do not give the vote count.
- **District's stated reason:** The August 2026 forecast shows the General Fund balance (Line 10.010) falling from **$32.19M at June 30, 2027 to −$3.72M at June 30, 2030** ([district levy-facts page, citing the Aug. 2026 forecast][1]).
- **Cuts already made:** The district plans to cut **$5 million** from the General Fund by June 30, 2027. It says **$3.4 million** of that is in place. On May 6, 2026 the board eliminated **32 positions** effective July 1, 2026. Contracts were suspended for **21 people**; the others moved into other jobs ([district, May 8, 2026][5]; [district levy-facts page][1]).
- **Last new operating levy:** November 2020, when voters approved a 4.8-mill levy ([district levy-facts page][1]).

## 1. What is on the ballot

### Ballot language (verified)

The certified language on the Board of Elections issues list reads:

> "An additional tax for the benefit of the Cleveland Heights – University Heights City School District for the purpose of current expenses that the County Fiscal Officer estimates will collect $11,100,193 annually, at a rate not exceeding 6.5 mills for each $1 of taxable value, which amounts to $228 for each $100,000 of the County Fiscal Officer's market value, for a continuing period of time, commencing in 2026, first due in calendar year 2027. The County Fiscal Officer has certified that the amount by which the carry-over balance in the district's general operating budget from the preceding fiscal year exceeds the district's general fund expenditures made in the preceding fiscal year is $0, which is 0% of those expenditures."
> — [Cuyahoga County BOE, Nov. 3, 2026 issues list][7]

The district's levy-facts page shows the same wording ([district][1]).

- **"Additional"** means new money on top of the district's existing levies.
- **"Continuing"** means the levy has no end date. Voters can still reduce or repeal it through a later ballot measure ([Cleveland 19][2]).
- **The "$0 carry-over" sentence** is a required disclosure. It means the district's carry-over balance was **not** larger than a full year of General Fund spending. It does not mean the district had no money left.

### How much it would cost

Ohio taxes homes on 35% of market value. One mill is $1 of tax for every $1,000 of that taxed value ([Cuyahoga County Auditor][8]). New levies passed after 2013 do not get the state's 10% and 2.5% rollbacks, so the homeowner pays the full amount (general Ohio law; not re-verified for this page).

| Home market value (County Fiscal Officer) | OpenHeights calculation (6.5 mills × 35%) | Official figure |
|---|---|---|
| $100,000 | 6.5 × 0.35 × 100,000 ÷ 1,000 = **$227.50/yr** | **$228/yr** ([BOE][7]) |
| $150,000 | **$341.25/yr** | — |
| $200,000 | **$455.00/yr** | — |
| $300,000 | **$682.50/yr** | — |

Use the market value on the County Fiscal Officer's record for your property, not a recent sale price.

**Tax base check (OpenHeights calculation):** $11,100,193 ÷ 0.0065 = **$1,707.7 million** of taxable (35%) value. That matches the district's stated calendar-2026 property value of **$1,707,722,020** in its August 2026 forecast assumptions ([Aug. 2026 assumptions][41]). The county's revenue estimate is the full 6.5 mills on today's tax base.

### What the money is for

The district says the levy supports day-to-day General Fund costs: teaching, staff, special education, transportation, technology, utilities and maintenance ([district][1]; [Cleveland 19][2]). Operating levy money cannot pay for building projects. Those are paid for by bond issues such as the 2013 Issue 81 ([district Bond Issue 81 page][10]).

### What happens if it fails

- **The district's own numbers:** Without new money, the August 2026 forecast shows the General Fund balance below zero by June 30, 2030 ([district][1]). Ohio districts cannot legally end a fiscal year with a negative balance, so the forecast's negative years show what would have to be closed by cuts or new revenue.
- **District officials' statements:** Board President Gabe Crenshaw said in July 2026 that "without a levy, our students' instruction will suffer." Treasurer Angéle Latham called 6.5 mills "the minimum amount needed to sufficiently cover the costs to operate the District and avoid additional budget cuts" ([district, July 27, 2026][39]).
- **No published cut list.** We found **no district document listing specific cuts** if Issue 4 fails. A Heights Observer column by a levy supporter says opportunities "will be reduced to the basics in the 2028–29 school year" without the levy ([Heights Observer, Sept. 28, 2026][42]). That is an opinion piece, not a district plan.
- **History, not a forecast:** After the spring 2020 levy failed, the district cut 24 positions, 8 of them teachers ([Cleveland Jewish News, 2020][12]).

## 2. The forecast

### What the forecast is, and which versions exist

Every Ohio district must file a General Fund forecast with the state twice a year and may file extra updates (ORC 5705.391; [Ohio DEW forecast page][15]). Each one shows three years of actual results plus projections. **Since the FY2026 cycle, CH-UH's filings project the current year plus three more years (four projected years), not five.** The state data files leave the old fifth-year column empty ([state bulk forecast files][43]).

**Treasurer's stated assumptions, August 2026** ([assumptions PDF][41]):

- **Property values:** $1,707.7M for calendar year 2026, up 26% from $1,256.2M in calendar year 2024 because of the sexennial reappraisal and new construction. Under HB 920 the reappraisal does not raise voted-levy revenue by the same percentage.
- **State aid:** FY2026–27 are the last two years of the Fair School Funding Plan phase-in under HB 96. State funding falls despite the phase-in, because base-cost inputs remain at FY2022 costs and higher property values reduce the state share. FY2028 and later assume "modest increases."
- **Salaries:** a 3.25% cost-of-living raise each year from FY2026 through FY2028, per the spring 2025 union contracts. The reduction in force effective July 1, 2026 saves **$2.42M** in salaries in FY2027.
- **Benefits:** retirement costs rise 3.25% a year. Health insurance rises 7.5% in FY2026, partly offset by dropping GLP-1 weight-loss coverage, then **10% a year**.
- **Other lines:** purchased services (12.09% of spending) grow about 2% a year. Supplies are $3.79M in FY2027, growing 2%. Capital outlay is $0.78M, growing 3%. Other objects (memberships, county auditor fees) are about $2.9M, flat. Other revenue is $8.14M in FY2027, including $2.06M of interest and $4.55M of tuition and special-education reimbursement.

The August forecast's FY2027 balance is **$8.5M higher** than February's. The district attributes part of the change to the reduction in force. Its board posts put the cut savings at about $3.4M ([district][5]); the forecast credits $2.42M to salaries. We could not get the full August line items, so we cannot fully explain the improvement. The rest presumably reflects FY2026 actual results coming in better than projected, but that is our inference.

**Year-to-year drop in the August balance (OpenHeights calculation):** FY27→FY28 −$7.38M; FY28→FY29 −$11.71M; FY29→FY30 −$16.82M. That is the yearly shortfall if encumbrances stay flat. The gap widens each year, because spending (mainly benefits at 10% a year) grows faster than revenue.

#### A data problem in the state's August file

The state's bulk data file for the FY2027 first-required filing lists CH-UH's submission as dated Aug. 26, 2026. But its numbers are **identical, line for line, to the February 2026 filing** (FY2023–FY2029, ending at −$13.56M). They do not match the FY2024–FY2030 figures in the district's August assumptions document or levy page. CH-UH was the only one of 659 districts in both files whose August balance row matched its February row exactly (OpenHeights check of [FY2027 file][49] against [FY2026 required update file][47]). We cannot tell whether the district uploaded old numbers or the state file has an error. This page uses the district's published August figures for August 2026.

### Resolving the deficit-date conflict: FY2029 vs. FY2030

Both dates are correct. They come from different forecast versions.

| Source | Forecast it reflects | When balance goes negative |
|---|---|---|
| Oct. 2025 and Feb. 2026 forecasts | Before the May 2026 cuts | Cash **and** Line 10.010 negative in **FY2029** (school year 2028–29): cash −$10.56M, balance −$13.56M (Feb.) |
| Superintendent Kirby, May 2026: cash could run out "by the 2028 to 2029 academic year" ([Hoodline, May 2026][11]) | Matches the Feb. 2026 forecast | FY2029 |
| Hoodline, Sept. 2026: "overall cash balance expected to turn negative by fiscal year 2029" ([Hoodline][3]) | Repeats the earlier figure | FY2029 |
| August 2026 forecast ([district][1]) | After the cuts and FY2026 actual results | Line 10.010 negative in **FY2030** (−$3.72M) |

**Cash vs. fund balance.** Line 7.020 is the actual **cash** at June 30. Line 10.010 is that cash minus money already committed to purchase orders (encumbrances, Line 8.010) and any required reserves. CH-UH has no reserves, and its forecasts assume $3.0M of encumbrances each year. So Line 10.010 is about $3M below cash. In the February forecast: −$10.56M cash − $3.00M = −$13.56M. If the August forecast uses the same $3.0M assumption, its FY2030 cash would be about **−$0.7M** (OpenHeights calculation: −3.72 + 3.00). We could not confirm the August encumbrance figure. Line 10.010 is the figure the law uses to certify that spending is covered, and it is the one the district cites.

### How sensitive is the deficit date?

- **Health insurance:** benefits are $32–40M a year and assumed to grow 10% a year. Each percentage point is roughly $0.3–0.4M a year (OpenHeights estimate).
- **Raises:** salaries are about $71–79M. Each 1% raise is about $0.7–0.8M a year. Contracts after FY2028 are not settled.
- **State aid:** the district says the incomplete Fair School Funding Plan phase-in cost it about **$7M over two years** ([district, May 8, 2026][5]). That is about the size of one year's shortfall in the August forecast. The next state budget (FY2028–29) is the largest unknown.
- **Forecast revisions:** the FY2029 balance moved by $26.7M between February and August 2026. Forecasts this far out change a lot.

### Other financial context

- **EdChoice.** The district says it is "still recovering from losing millions of dollars to EdChoice vouchers" ([district][39]). Reported past deductions were **$4.2M in 2019** and **$6.8M estimated for the following year** ([Patch][16]). Since HB 110 (2021) the state pays EdChoice directly instead of deducting it from district aid ([NEPC][17]). Pre-2021 figures do not apply directly to the current forecast. See the EdChoice page in this guide.
- **State aid trend.** Combined state aid fell from **$15.85M (FY2024) to $13.01M (FY2026)** ([Aug. 2026 assumptions][41]).

## 4. Tax burden and why voted levies don't grow with home values

### HB 920 and the 20-mill floor

Ohio's HB 920 reduction factors cut the effective rate on voted levies when existing property is reappraised. Each levy then collects about the same dollars it did when it passed ([district FY2025 ACFR][50]; [Moore primer][27]). New construction adds revenue; rising values on existing homes do not. Reduction factors cannot push a district's effective current-expense rate below **20 mills**. Districts at that "floor" do gain revenue as values rise ([CCAO][30]; [OSBA][31]).

### CH-UH's millage (verified)

| Measure (tax year 2024, collected 2025) | CH-UH | Similar-district average | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voted current operating millage | **149.50 mills** | 66.09 | [DEW FY2025 District Profile Report][53] |
| **Effective residential (Class 1) operating millage** | **51.05 mills** | 28.09 | [DEW][53] |
| Effective commercial (Class 2) operating millage | 84.63 mills | 40.97 | [DEW][53] |
| Total voted school millage, all funds | 159.80 (149.50 operating + 3.80 permanent improvement + 6.50 bond) | — | [FY2025 ACFR, Table 8][50] |

- **CH-UH is far above the 20-mill floor.** Its effective residential operating rate is 51.05 mills. HB 186's limit on revenue growth for floor districts ([LSC][32]) does not apply to it directly.
- **Rank (OpenHeights calculation from the DEW file):** CH-UH has the **2nd-highest** effective residential operating millage of Cuyahoga County's 31 districts, after Shaker Heights (63.62). The county median is 35.41. It ranks **4th of 606** Ohio districts; the state median is 22.86 mills. In this file, 161 districts sit at exactly 20 mills.
- **What Issue 4 adds:** 6.5 new mills, collected at full rate in the first year. That is about **12.7%** more residential operating millage (OpenHeights calculation: 6.5 ÷ 51.05).

### Total property tax in the Heights

- **County rate table, tax year 2024:** Cleveland Heights' total residential rate was **205.25 voted / 92.30 effective mills**. That is tax equal to **2.92%** of market value. University Heights' was 206.03 / 93.08 (**2.94%**) ([County Fiscal Officer, Tax Rates by Community TY2024][35]). These totals include all taxing bodies: schools, city, county, library and others. We did not find a TY2025 table.
- The County Auditor says Cuyahoga has Ohio's highest average effective property tax rate ([Auditor][8]).
- The City of Cleveland Heights lists a total effective rate of $117.68 per $1,000 of assessed value, undated ([city][54]). It does not match the county's TY2024 figure, so it is probably from an earlier year. We prefer the county table.
- A Strongsville treasurer reported a county average of about **40.4 mills** for 2025 school residential rates ([Citizen Portal][33]). That measure appears to include bond and permanent-improvement millage, so it is not directly comparable with the DEW operating figures above.

## 5. Arguments and campaigns

**Opposition (verified active in 2026):** The PAC **Tiger Nation 4 Lower Taxes**, which opposed the 2020 levies ([CJN][12]; [Ideastream][23]), has mailed a 2026 flyer about Issue 4. The flyer compares CH-UH's administrator salaries, administrative cost per student, operating spending per student and property tax per pupil with "similar districts" in the state District Profile Report. This is according to a letter writer who said the flyer's numbers "are correct" but incomplete ([Heights Observer, Oct. 12, 2026][55]). We did not see the flyer itself.

**Support:** A levy campaign is active. A Heights Observer column names parent Emily Lampe of Parents United for Public Schools as working on it ([Heights Observer, Sept. 28, 2026][42]). A former board member writing in support volunteers with the Heights Coalition for Public Education ([Heights Observer, Sept. 28, 2026][56]). **We did not find the name of the official campaign committee or any 2026 campaign-finance filings.**

### What supporters argue

- **The deficit is in the district's state-filed forecast.** Every 2025–26 version shows the balance going negative by FY2029 or FY2030 ([forecasts above](#2-the-forecast)).
- **Cuts came first.** 32 positions eliminated and $3.4M saved before the levy request ([district][5]).
- **State decisions drive much of the gap.** State aid fell from $15.85M to $13.01M between FY2024 and FY2026 ([Aug. 2026 assumptions][41]). The district says the incomplete Fair School Funding Plan cost it about $7M over two years ([district][5]). Supporters note the state had a budget surplus in 2026 ([Heights Observer][56]).
- **Context for cost comparisons.** Compared with "similar districts," CH-UH has more experienced teachers, more students with disabilities (22% vs. 18%), more economically disadvantaged students, and less state revenue per pupil ($4,933 vs. $6,073). This is according to a letter citing the DEW profile report ([Heights Observer][55]). Its operating spending per pupil ($27,676) is close to Shaker Heights' ([DEW][53]; [Heights Observer][55]).
- **HB 920 keeps old levies flat.** Districts above the 20-mill floor must return to voters to keep up with costs ([ACFR][50]).

### What skeptics argue

- **The tax burden is already among Ohio's highest.** CH-UH's effective residential operating rate (51.05 mills) is 4th-highest of 606 Ohio districts and 2nd in Cuyahoga County ([DEW][53]; OpenHeights ranking). Issue 4 would add about 12.7%.
- **Costs are high compared with similar districts.** The opposition PAC cites the state profile's figures for administrator pay and spending per pupil ([Heights Observer][55]). Total property tax per pupil was $20,764 for CH-UH vs. $9,699 for similar districts in TY2024 ([DEW][53]). Total operating spending per pupil was $27,676 in FY2025 ([DEW][53]). Critics have long argued the district has "a spending, not funding, problem" ([Heights Observer][36]).
- **The forecast's own assumptions show the gap returning.** Benefits grow 10% a year. Even with Issue 4, the August forecast implies renewed deficit spending by FY2030 (OpenHeights illustration above). Skeptics can argue for further cost control first.
- **A continuing levy has no end date** ([Cleveland 19][2]).
- **Forecasts move a lot.** The FY2029 balance improved by $26.7M between February and August 2026. Skeptics can argue the "zero date" may keep moving.
- **Fixed-income homeowners** bear rising bills; new levies get no state rollback ([Patch][29]).
