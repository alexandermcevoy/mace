# Governance, Leadership, and a Timeline of Recent Events

*Cleveland Heights-University Heights City School District (CH-UH), Ohio, IRN 043687. Research compiled 2026-10-12. Every statement below links to its source. Items we could not confirm are listed under "Gaps / unverified." Bracketed numbers refer to the numbered source list at the end.*

## Key facts

- **Superintendent:** Elizabeth (Liz) Kirby. She has led the district since August 1, 2019, and was still giving superintendent reports at the September 1, 2026 board meeting ([chuh.org, Sept. 1, 2026][5]; [Heights Observer, Mar. 2019][11]).
- **Chief Financial Officer/Treasurer:** Angele Latham, who started July 1, 2025. She succeeded Scott Gainer, who retired effective July 31, 2025 after about 30 years in the district ([chuh.org, June 2025][4]).
- **2026 Board of Education:** Gabe Crenshaw (president), Phil Trimble (vice president), Kathi Howard-Primes (treasurer pro tem), Dan Heintz, and Tom Hodgkiss-Lilly ([chuh.org, Jan. 6, 2026][1]).
- **November 4, 2025 board election:** Three at-large seats were filled by Kathi Howard-Primes (about 32.9%), incumbent Dan Heintz (about 30.4%) and Tom Hodgkiss-Lilly (about 27.8%). Rakhel Davidoff received about 8.9%. Incumbents Malia Lewis and Jodi Sourini did not run again ([Cleveland Jewish News, Nov. 2025][2]; [chuh.org, Jan. 6, 2026][1]).
- **Issue 4 (Nov. 3, 2026):** A 6.5-mill continuing operating levy. The district estimates it would raise about $11.1 million a year and cost $228 a year per $100,000 of county market value. It is the district's first request for new operating money since a 4.8-mill levy in November 2020 ([Cleveland19, Oct. 7, 2026][9]; [Hoodline, Sept. 2026][10]).
- **Budget cuts:** The district planned at least $5 million in cuts over two years. On May 6, 2026 the board eliminated 32 positions, which led to 21 layoffs. The district cited incomplete funding of the Fair School Funding Plan, lower state aid after property values rose, and EdChoice voucher losses ([chuh.org][7]; [WKYC, 2026][8]).
- **State audit:** In May 2021 the Ohio Auditor of State issued a $34,675 finding for recovery over district-funded polling about a levy ([Ohio Auditor of State, May 11, 2021][22]).

## Narrative

### Leadership stability since 2019

Since Kirby's hiring in 2019, the district has had a stable top administration. Kirby has served about seven years. Treasurer Scott Gainer served about 30 years before retiring in 2025 ([chuh.org][4]). Before that, leadership changed more often. Dixon took the Columbus City Schools job in 2018, less than five years after arriving, and an interim superintendent served until Kirby started ([WOSU][14]; [Patch][13]). When hired, Kirby was described as a leader who would not push a "restart button" ([Heights Observer][11]). We found no public reporting on a contract extension for Kirby. See Gaps.

### Board composition and 2025 election

In the 2025 election, two seats went to newcomers (Howard-Primes and Hodgkiss-Lilly) and one long-serving incumbent (Heintz) returned. Lewis, a longtime president, and Sourini left the board ([CJN][2]; [chuh.org][1]). All five members are elected at large. The current majority's terms run to 2027 (Crenshaw, Trimble) or 2029 (the 2025 winners) ([BallotReady][43]).

### Labor relations

**Note on the research brief:** The brief mentioned a 2022–23 strike authorization. We found no evidence of one. The documented dispute took place in **November–December 2020**. The board imposed its final offer, which the union said would raise health premiums and cut other compensation; the union said the cost to members would be $3,000–$5,000 a year. The Cleveland Heights Teachers Union, with about 500 members, gave notice of a December 2 strike. A tentative deal reached in an overnight session prevented the strike, and members ratified it with 93% approval ([News5][27]; [WKYC][26]; [CJN][28]). Since then, a 2022 agreement filed with the State Employment Relations Board included 2% raises effective July 1, 2024 ([SERB][29]). The current agreement covers July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2028 ([CHTU CBA][30]). We could not confirm the raise schedule in the 2025–28 agreement.

### Facilities

Voters approved the $134.8 million Issue 81 bond in November 2013 after a contentious planning process. In 2012 the board approved "Plan C" 3–2, after an earlier rejection and a citizen Lay Facilities Committee process ([Patch][19]; [Heights Observer][20]; [CJN][17]). The bond paid for renovating Heights High and the Monticello and Roxboro middle schools. Heritage Ohio describes the renovated Heights High as about 130,000 square feet of renovated space plus 235,000 square feet of new construction, serving about 1,600 students ([Heritage Ohio][18]). Wiley served as swing space during construction and has been vacant since the middle schools reopened in 2018. As of March 2024 its future had not been decided ([Heights Observer][40]).

**Former buildings:**

- **Coventry:** Sold to Heights Libraries for $1 in March 2018, after the district said it needed more than $1 million in repairs. The library now plans to demolish it to expand PEACE Park ([Cleveland Scene][36]; [Hoodline][37]).
- **Millikin:** Closed in 2006. A 2014 agreement to sell it to Mosdos Ohr Hatorah for $650,000 fell apart in early 2015. Cleveland Heights council members and the mayor at the time had supported that sale, and there was a public dispute over appraisals: $2.4 million in 2005 versus a later figure of $770,000. In 2020 the city made a preliminary offer to market the property ([CJN][38]; [Heights Observer blog][39]).
- **Taylor Academy:** Converted around 2015 into the Delisle Educational Options Center ([Heights Observer][41]).

We found no evidence of a 2024–2026 building closure or reconfiguration plan. See Gaps.

### Finances and accountability (governance angle)

Two operating levies came in 2020. The April 2020 levy failed, and the November 2020 levy passed by about 0.2% ([CJN][24]; [CJN][25]). The state's 2021 finding for recovery held that paying with public money for a poll of "levy voters" violated Ohio law. Kirby approved the polling questions. The finding was issued jointly against the vendor, Kirby, the treasurer and his bonding company ([Ohio Auditor][22]; [Richland Source][23]). We did not find a record of repayment. In 2026 the district announced at least $5 million in cuts over two years before placing Issue 4 on the ballot ([chuh.org][7]; [WKYC][8]). Other OpenHeights pages cover district finances in depth.

### Strategic plan

The 2026 plan, "Excellence For All," has five goals: student outcomes, educational approach, family and community engagement, valued professionals, and operational resources. Its core values are Excellence, Equity, Integrity, Trust and Respect. The plan followed the earlier "Heights 2020" plan, and the COVID-19 pandemic delayed its update ([chuh.org][42]).

### Relationship with the cities and library

- **Cedar-Lee-Meadowbrook (2022):** Then-Mayor Kahlil Seren publicly predicted the board would reject the TIF over an unrelated dispute about sharing facilities. The board approved it unanimously ([CJN][33]; [CJN][31]; [chuh.org][32]).
- **City leadership change:** Seren was recalled in September 2025. Jim Petras was elected mayor in November 2025 and took office January 1, 2026 ([Ideastream][34]; [Ideastream][35]).
- **Heights Libraries:** The library's purchase of the Coventry building is the main joint-property precedent ([Cleveland Scene][36]).
