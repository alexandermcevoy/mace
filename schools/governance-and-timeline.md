# Governance, Leadership, and a Timeline of Recent Events

[← Back to the CH-UH schools guide](./)

*Cleveland Heights-University Heights City School District (CH-UH), Ohio, IRN 043794. Research compiled 2026-10-12. Every statement below links to its source. Items we could not confirm are listed under "Gaps / unverified." Bracketed numbers refer to the numbered source list at the end.*

> **Verification status: checked against primary sources on Oct. 12, 2026**: the district's board page and BoardDocs meeting records (agendas, resolutions and roll-call votes, 2017–2026), Cuyahoga County Board of Elections certified results, and Ohio Auditor of State audit reports and fiscal-distress lists. The exceptions are items listed under Gaps / unverified. A few older facilities and city-relations items still rest on news reports, and they are labeled that way.

## Key facts

- **Superintendent:** Elizabeth (Liz) Kirby, since August 1, 2019. She was hired on a three-year contract at a starting salary of $175,000. In November 2021 the board extended her contract through **July 31, 2027**. BoardDocs shows no later extension vote through October 2026 ([BoardDocs, June 4, 2019][b1]; [BoardDocs, Nov. 3, 2021][b2]).
- **Chief Financial Officer/Treasurer:** Angèle Latham. The board approved her appointment on June 3, 2025. She began July 1, 2025 under a transition contractor agreement, and she was formally appointed CFO/Treasurer effective **August 1, 2025** for three years, through July 31, 2028, at a starting salary of $170,000. She succeeded Scott Gainer, who retired effective July 31, 2025 after 30 years ([chuh.org, June 6, 2025][4]; [BoardDocs, June 3, 2025][b3]).
- **2026 Board of Education:** Gabe Crenshaw (president, term ends Dec. 31, 2027), Phil Trimble (vice president, 2027), Kathi Howard-Primes (treasurer pro tem, 2029), Dan Heintz (2029), and Thomas Hodgkiss-Lilly (2029) ([chuh.org board page][bp]; [chuh.org, Jan. 7, 2026][1]).
- **November 4, 2025 board election (certified):** Three seats were filled. Kathi Howard-Primes received 9,712 votes (32.95%), Dan Heintz 8,954 (30.38%), Tom Hodgkiss-Lilly 8,188 (27.78%) and Rakhel Davidoff 2,623 (8.90%). Incumbents Malia Lewis and Jodi Sourini did not run again, and their terms ended Dec. 31, 2025 ([Cuyahoga BOE official results, Nov. 19, 2025][e25]; [chuh.org][1]).
- **Issue 4 (Nov. 3, 2026):** A 6.5-mill continuing levy for current expenses. The board voted 5–0 on August 3, 2026 to place it on the ballot. The district estimates it would raise about $11.1 million a year and cost $228 a year per $100,000 of home market value. It is the district's first new operating levy request since the November 2020 4.8-mill levy ([BoardDocs, Aug. 3, 2026][b4]; [Cleveland19, Oct. 7, 2026][9]).
- **Budget cuts:** On May 6, 2026 the board eliminated 32 positions, about 3% of the workforce, effective July 1. Because staff could "bump" into other jobs, 21 employees' contracts were suspended. The district estimates the general-fund savings at about $3.4 million, part of a plan for at least $5 million in cuts over two years ([chuh.org, May 8, 2026][7]).
- **State audits:** The Auditor of State's FY2020 audit, released May 11, 2021, issued a $34,675 finding for recovery over district-paid levy polling. The finding was **repaid on July 23, 2021** by the district's insurer, the Schools of Ohio Risk Sharing Authority. Audits for FY2021 through FY2025 reported no findings, with clean opinions every year. The district does not appear on the Auditor's lists of school districts in fiscal watch or fiscal emergency, current or past ([AOS FY2020 report][a20]; [AOS FY2021 report][a21]; [AOS Schools in Fiscal Distress, Sept. 2026][fd]).

## Narrative

### Leadership stability since 2019

The district's top administration has been stable since 2019. Kirby has been superintendent for more than seven years. Gainer served as treasurer for about 30 years before retiring in 2025 ([chuh.org][4]). Before that, leadership changed faster. Dixon left for Columbus in early 2019 after about five years, and an interim superintendent served until Kirby arrived ([BoardDocs][b6]). The board described Kirby as someone who would not push a "restart button" ([Heights Observer][11]).

Kirby's current contract runs through **July 31, 2027**. Under Ohio law (ORC 3319.01), a board must act by March 1 of the year a superintendent's contract expires, either renewing it or giving written notice of nonrenewal. Otherwise the superintendent is automatically re-employed for one year. As of October 2026, BoardDocs shows no extension vote. The board has held several executive sessions in 2026 on "employment ... or compensation of an employee or official," but the agendas do not name who was discussed ([BoardDocs, 2026 agendas][bdl]). Her current salary is not in the board records we reviewed. See Gaps.

### Board composition and elections

In 2023, two seats drew only two candidates, Crenshaw and Trimble ([BOE][e23]). In 2025, three seats drew four candidates. Two newcomers (Howard-Primes and Hodgkiss-Lilly) won along with incumbent Heintz, and Howard-Primes was the top vote-getter ([BOE][e25]). Turnout in the district's board race was 36.75% in 2025 and 50.70% in 2023 ([BOE 2025][e25]; [BOE 2023][e23]). Heintz, sworn in for his third term in January 2026, is the longest-serving current member ([chuh.org][1]). Every 2026 roll call we checked, including the levy votes, was unanimous ([BoardDocs][b4]).

### Labor relations

The documented labor dispute was in **November–December 2020**. The board imposed its final offer. The union said the offer would raise health premiums to 250% of the current rate and cut other pay by 1%, costing members $3,000 to $5,000 a year. The district said it faced an $8 million deficit in FY2023 without cost savings ([CJN][28]; [News5][27]). The Cleveland Heights Teachers Union gave notice of a Dec. 2 strike. Overnight talks produced a tentative agreement at 6:30 a.m. that day, after some teachers had already begun picketing. The district called it a strike "averted." The last district strike was in 1983 ([CJN, Dec. 2, 2020][28b]; [CJN][28]). The board approved the agreement on Dec. 8, 2020 ([BoardDocs][b9]). In March 2022 a memorandum of understanding extended the teachers' contract through June 30, 2025 ([BoardDocs][b11]). The current agreement, approved June 24, 2025, runs from July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2028 and provides a **3.25% raise in each of its three years**. The bachelor's-degree starting salary rises from $49,277 (2025–26) to $50,879 (2026–27) and $52,533 (2027–28) ([CHTU CBA 2025–28, Appendix B][30]; [BoardDocs][b15]).

### Facilities

The 2012 administration plan ("Plan C," $206.2 million) passed 3–2 on July 3, 2012. Board members Ron Register and Kal Zucker voted no. The plan called for renovating the district in phases, ending with the elementary schools, and for closing three elementary buildings ([Heights Observer][20]; [Patch][19]). No CH-UH bond was on the November 2012 ballot ([BOE][e12]). A Lay Facilities Committee then produced a $234 million plan for 8 of the 11 buildings. Voters approved Phase I as Issue 81 in November 2013: a $134.8 million bond, a 38-year term and a $153.6 million total budget, with most of the difference financed by a bank loan. It covered Heights High and the Monticello and Roxboro middle schools ([Patch][21]; [BOE][e13]). Heritage Ohio describes the renovated Heights High as about 130,000 square feet of renovated space plus 235,000 square feet of new construction ([Heritage Ohio][18]). The district's 2025 facilities inventory lists Heights High (2017) at 372,334 square feet ([BoardDocs][b14]).

**Later phases have not happened.** The district's May 2025 facilities presentation lists seven elementary buildings built between 1920 and 1974: Boulevard (1974), Canterbury (1927), Fairfax (1974), Gearity (1954), Noble (1922), Oxford (1928) and Roxboro Elementary (1920). The plan relies on a 3.8-mill permanent improvement levy passed in 2002, which raises about $3.6 million a year, of which $586,400 goes to repaying certificates of participation. The district reported $129.6 million in outstanding debt against a 9% debt limit of $153.3 million, leaving about $23.7 million of capacity ([BoardDocs, May 20, 2025][b14]). We found no board-adopted plan from 2024 to 2026 to close, consolidate or rebuild elementary schools.

**Former and vacant buildings:**

- **Wiley** (1954, 147,819 sq. ft.): Closed as a middle school in 2015 and used as swing space (about $13 million). It has had no students since 2018. A March 2024 Heights Observer column argued the board should reuse or sell it ([Heights Observer][40]). In 2022, University Heights proposed a city service yard at Wiley, and the board awarded a bid for a bus depot on the Wiley site ([BoardDocs, May 3 and Aug. 2, 2022][b17]).
- **Coventry:** Transferred to Heights Libraries for $1 in March 2018, after the district said it needed more than $1 million in repairs. The library now plans to demolish it to expand PEACE Park ([Hoodline][37]).
- **Millikin** (1953): Closed in 2006. A 2014 agreement to sell it to Mosdos Ohr Hatorah for $650,000 collapsed in January 2015. Then-Mayor Dennis Wilcox said the city had hoped the sale would go through ([CJN][38]). The district still lists it as a district facility with no classrooms ([BoardDocs][b14]).
- **Taylor Academy:** Renamed and reopened in 2011 as the Deborah S. Delisle Educational Options Center ([Heights Observer][41]).

### Finances and accountability (governance angle)

The district put two operating levies on the ballot in 2020. The April levy (7.9 mills) failed with 47.3% of the vote. The November levy (4.8 mills) passed by 135 votes ([BOE][e20p]; [BOE][e20g]).

The state's FY2020 audit found that paying public money for a poll of "levy voters" violated Ohio law. Kirby approved the poll questions, and Treasurer Gainer authorized the payment. The finding for recovery was issued jointly and severally against the vendor (R Strategy Group), Kirby, Gainer and his bonding company. The audit report notes that officials did not respond ([AOS press release][22]; [AOS FY2020 report][a20]). The FY2021 audit lists the finding as "Fully Corrected": the district's insurance pool, the Schools of Ohio Risk Sharing Authority, repaid it on July 23, 2021 ([AOS FY2021 report][a21]). An earlier, unrelated finding in the FY2018 audit was $1,994 for a miscalculated severance payment to an employee, which the employee repaid during the audit ([AOS FY2018 report][a18]).

Recent audits are clean. For FY2021 and FY2022, the Auditor of State issued unmodified opinions with no findings. For FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025, Rea & Associates conducted the audits under contract with the Auditor of State, again with unmodified opinions, no reported findings, and "low-risk auditee" status for federal programs ([AOS FY2021][a21]; [AOS FY2022][a22]; [AOS FY2023][a23]; [AOS FY2024][a24]; [AOS FY2025][a25]). In the FY2025 audit, net position was restated for a new accounting standard (GASB 101) ([AOS FY2025][a25]).

**Fiscal distress:** As of September 2026, CH-UH is not on the Auditor of State's list of school districts in fiscal emergency or fiscal watch, and it does not appear among districts released from either status since 1996 ([AOS Schools in Fiscal Distress, Sept. 2026][fd]). Fiscal caution is declared by the Ohio Department of Education and Workforce, and we did not find that agency's current list. See Gaps.

In May 2026 the district said it had been drawing on cash reserves since 2024–25 and projected a negative $6 million cash balance by 2028–29 even after the planned cuts (district claim) ([chuh.org][7]). Other OpenHeights pages cover district finances in depth.

### Strategic plan

The board approved the current five-year strategic plan on Sept. 8, 2021 ([BoardDocs][b10]). The district web page titled "2021-2026 Strategic Plan" lists core values of Excellence, Equity, Integrity, Trust and Respect ([chuh.org][42]). District presentations refer to it as the "Five-Year Strategic Plan 2026." Its Goal Five is to "ensure that the District has the necessary finances and infrastructure to meet its student outcome goals" ([BoardDocs][b14]). Fall 2026 board summaries refer to "the seven goals of the District's One Plan." One Plan is the state's school-improvement planning framework ([chuh.org, Sept. 2, 2026][5]; [BoardDocs, Sept. 16, 2026][b18]). We did not find a newly adopted successor strategic plan.

### Heights High: selected recent programs and recognitions

- **AP:** 84 students were named AP Scholars for 2025–26: 40 AP Scholars, 17 with Honor and 27 with Distinction (district announcement) ([chuh.org][47]).
- **Associate Pathway (College Credit Plus with Tri-C):** In fall 2026, 25 juniors started on-campus courses at Tri-C East. They join 8 seniors, 12 sophomores and 11 freshmen earning credit toward an associate degree (district report) ([BoardDocs, Sept. 1, 2026][b19]).
- **State recognition:** Heights High and Baumgardner Middle School received the Ohio Department of Education and Workforce "Momentum" award, recognized at the May 6, 2026 board meeting ([chuh.org][6]).
- **Report card (district presentation):** The district's overall rating was 2.5 stars in 2023–24 and 3.5 in both 2024–25 and 2025–26 ([BoardDocs, Sept. 16, 2026][b18]). See the OpenHeights academics page for state data.

### Relationship with the cities and library

- **Cedar-Lee-Meadowbrook (2022):** In an Oct. 21 letter, then-Mayor Kahlil Seren said he believed the board would reject the school compensation agreement because of an unrelated dispute over sharing facilities. On Oct. 26 the board unanimously approved the TIF, the compensation agreement and a parking agreement. Board President Lewis read a facilities-sharing clause she said Seren had agreed to ([CJN][33]; [CJN][31]).
- **City leadership change:** Seren was recalled in September 2025. Jim Petras was elected mayor in November 2025 and took office Jan. 1, 2026 ([Ideastream][34]; [Ideastream][35]).
- **Heights Libraries:** The $1 Coventry transfer is the main joint-property precedent ([Hoodline][37]).
