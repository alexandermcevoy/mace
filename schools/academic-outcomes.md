# Academic Outcomes: The Report Card Record

[← Back to the CH-UH schools guide](./)

*Cleveland Heights-University Heights City School District (CH-UH). Research current to October 12, 2026. Issue 4, a 6.5-mill continuing operating levy, is on the November 3, 2026 ballot. This page covers the district's academic record only. Finances, demographics and a demographically-adjusted analysis are on separate pages.*

> **Verification status: checked against primary sources on Oct. 12, 2026**, except items listed under [Gaps / unverified](#gaps--unverified). Ratings, Performance Index, graduation, early literacy, proficiency, subgroup, absenteeism and college/career figures below come from the Ohio Department of Education and Workforce (DEW) report card data files and the data behind the state's report card website for district IRN 043794 [1][2]. District statements are labeled as the district's. Several figures in the first draft were corrected; see the [Corrections log](#corrections-log).

## Key facts

- **Overall rating, 2025-26: 3.5 of 5 stars**, the same as 2024-25. The overall ratings since Ohio began issuing them were **3 stars (2022-23), 2.5 (2023-24), 3.5 (2024-25) and 3.5 (2025-26)** [2]. In Ohio's system, 3 stars or more means "meets state standards," so the district fell below that line only in 2023-24.
- **Statewide context:** the average Ohio district earned 3.78 stars overall in 2025-26 and the median district earned 4. Of 607 districts, 51.6% rated above CH-UH's 3.5, 23.9% matched it and 24.5% rated below it (*OpenHeights calculation* from the state's district file [2]).
- **Performance Index (PI): 66.9 (2021-22), 70.6, 69.2, 74.6, 79.0 (2025-26)** [2][3]. The 2025-26 PI is 3.8 points below the statewide PI (82.8) and 9.5 points below the median Ohio district (88.5). It was higher than the PI of 17.8% of districts, up from 6.6% in 2021-22 (*OpenHeights calculation* [3]).
- **Component ratings, 2025-26:** Achievement 3, Progress 4, Gap Closing 5, Graduation 3, Early Literacy 3, College/Career/Workforce/Military Readiness 3 [2]. Achievement (the passing-rate measure) was 2 stars every year from 2021-22 through 2024-25.
- **Graduation:** four-year rate **92.1%** (class of 2025) and five-year rate **94.3%** (class of 2024), against state rates of 88.8% and 90.1%. The weighted rate was 93.0% [2][8].
- **Third-grade reading:** 68.9% of third graders were proficient on the measure used in the Early Literacy component in 2025-26, up from 64.0% in 2024-25 [8].
- **Subject proficiency is below the state in most grades, especially middle-school math:** 32.3% of 8th graders were proficient in math in 2025-26, against 52.0% statewide. High school Algebra I (66.8% vs. 61.6%) and 3rd-grade ELA (69.7% vs. 63.4%) were above the state [3].
- **Racial gap:** in 2025-26, 47.8% of Black students and 91.6% of white students scored proficient or above in English language arts (a 43.8-point gap); in math the figures were 38.7% and 87.1% (48.4 points). Statewide the same gaps were 30.5 and 34.3 points. Both CH-UH groups score above their statewide counterparts, and CH-UH's gap has narrowed since 2023-24 [4][5] (*OpenHeights calculation*).
- **Chronic absenteeism: 37.9% in 2025-26**, against 23.9% statewide; it was 46.3% in 2021-22 [8].

## How to read Ohio's report card

- **The rating system changed, so older and newer grades don't line up.** Through 2011-12 Ohio gave districts designations such as "Effective," "Continuous Improvement" and "Academic Watch." From 2012-13 it graded measures A–F, and only in 2017-18 and 2018-19 did it give an overall letter grade. No ratings were issued for 2019-20 or 2020-21 because of COVID-19. Since 2021-22 Ohio rates components on a 1–5 star scale, and since 2022-23 it also gives an **overall** star rating in half-star steps [12][13]. The state data file for 2021-22 lists CH-UH's overall rating as "NR" (not rated) [2]. **A pre-2020 "D" and a post-2022 "2.5 stars" are not on the same scale.**
- **3 stars = "meets state standards."** 4 stars exceeds them, and 1–2 stars means the district needs support to meet them [13].
- **The six components:** Achievement (how well students score, summarized in the Performance Index); Progress (value-added growth compared with what was expected for those same students); Gap Closing (whether student groups meet targets, plus chronic absenteeism, English learner and gifted measures); Graduation (four- and five-year rates, weighted 60/40); Early Literacy (K–3 reading); and College, Career, Workforce & Military Readiness (CCWMR), first rated in 2024-25 [2][12].
- **Achievement and Progress measure different things.** Achievement is closely tied to students' family income and other background factors. Progress tries to measure the school's effect on students' growth. A district can score low on Achievement and high on Progress at the same time. CH-UH's Progress rating was one star above its Achievement rating in every year since 2021-22 except 2023-24, when both were 2 stars.
- **Performance Index.** Each tested student earns points by performance level, from 0 for "limited" up to 1.2 for "advanced plus," so the theoretical maximum PI is 120 [12]. The Achievement star rating is based on "PI percent," which divides a district's PI by the average PI of the top 2% of districts (110.2 in 2025-26), not by 120 [3].

*Example, 2025-26 ELA:* CH-UH 91.6 − 47.8 = 43.8; Ohio 69.7 − 39.2 = 30.5. Statewide 2025-26 proficiency was 39.2% (ELA) and 30.4% (math) for Black students and 69.7% and 64.7% for white students [5]. The state figures use Ohio's ESSA performance-level data, which differ slightly in method from the Gap Closing file; differences are generally under 1 point.

What the data show:

- **CH-UH's racial gap is about 13–14 points larger than the statewide gap** in both subjects in 2025-26.
- **Part of the reason is that CH-UH's white students score unusually high.** They were 21.9 points above white students statewide in ELA (91.6 vs. 69.7) and 22.4 points above in math (87.1 vs. 64.7). CH-UH's Black students were also above Black students statewide: by 8.6 points in ELA (47.8 vs. 39.2) and 8.3 in math (38.7 vs. 30.4) (*OpenHeights calculation*).
- **The gap has narrowed since 2023-24**, by 5.2 points in ELA and 4.1 in math, while both groups' scores rose. This supports the district's statement that "the achievement gap between white students and black students is closing, while student achievement for both groups continues to increase" [10]. Measured from 2021-22, the narrowing is smaller (4.6 points in ELA and 2.4 in math).
- **The 5-star Gap Closing rating does not measure the size of the gap.** It scores whether each student group meets an annual target or shows enough improvement, plus chronic absenteeism, English learner progress and gifted measures [12].
